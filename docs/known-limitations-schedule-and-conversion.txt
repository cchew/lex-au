Known limitations: schedule rendering and DOCX-to-AKN conversion
===============================================================

Companion to docs/known-limitations-empty-body.txt. Each entry is a standing,
accepted limitation in the v0.9.0 pipeline, not a regression the spot-check or
fidelity audit should keep re-flagging.


(a) Two Acts retain pre-v0.9.0 XML (stale _resolve_title apostrophe fallback)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Slugs:
  industrial-relations-court-(judges'-remuneration)-act-1993
  royal-australian-air-force-veterans'-residences-act-1953

The v0.9.0 `--force` re-ingest did not re-convert these two Acts. `_resolve_title`
has an apostrophe-handling fallback that fails to match the canonical
legislation.gov.au title for names containing a typographic apostrophe, so the
fetch step resolved no compilation and the build was skipped. Their committed XML
is therefore the pre-v0.9.0 output: no schedule tables, older reflinks placement.
Fixing `_resolve_title` needs its own patch (tracked separately); until then a
targeted re-convert of these two Acts is blocked.


(b) Prose after a schedule <paragraph>/<subparagraph> can attach above it
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
`_build_schedule_content` (src/lexau/builder.py) resets the running
`current_content` pointer to None after a clause boundary, a subclause boundary
and a TABLE element, so prose that follows those opens a fresh <content> in the
correct document position. The PARAGRAPH and SUBPARAGRAPH branches do NOT reset
`current_content`. If a schedule emits a <paragraph> (or <subparagraph>) and is
then followed by loose BODY prose, that prose is appended to the <content> that
was open before the <paragraph>, so it renders above the <paragraph> in document
order instead of after it.

Pre-existing, low incidence (schedules rarely interleave numbered paragraphs with
trailing loose prose), and distinct from the TABLE-reset path, which IS fixed.
Deferred; not addressed in the v0.9.0 fix wave.


(c) ParseReport.tables_found counts body tables only
---------------------------------------------------
`ParseReport.tables_found` is incremented only on the body-path TABLE handler.
Schedule tables, rendered by `_build_schedule_content` since v0.9.0, are not
counted. A ParseReport showing `tables_found: 0` for an Act that in fact has
schedule rate/tariff/repeal tables is expected, not a conversion loss. The audit
(`scripts/audit_conversion_fidelity.py`) measures rendered <td>/<th> cells
directly and is the authoritative count for schedule-table content.


(d) v0.9.0 corpus XML predates the _content_for parent-affinity fix
------------------------------------------------------------------
The C1 fix in `builder.py` `_build_schedule_content._content_for` (guard:
`current_content.getparent() is not parent`) landed AFTER the v0.9.0 `--force`
re-ingest. The committed v0.9.0 corpus XML was therefore built with the pre-fix
builder: in an as-yet unenumerated set of Acts, schedule `Note:` lines and block
headings sit under a clause-level <content> where they should be subclause-level
(or vice versa). Only the cite-eId of those notes/headings is wrong; no operative
subclause or paragraph text is affected and no text is lost or duplicated
(`prose_set_same` held on every measured Act).

Incidence sample: 1 of 13 schedule-bearing Acts (fast-path measurement, 15 Acts
sampled, `corporations-act-2001` not measured due to an unrelated lxml xpath
stall) -- `anti-terrorism-act-(no.-2)-2005`, 34 paragraphs in Schedule 4
clause 24. Extrapolates to low-thousands of schedule note/heading <p> elements
corpus-wide.

Fix path (not run at v0.9.0 tag time): run the fast-path detector over all
`hcontainer name="subclause"` Acts to produce the affected-Act list, then a
targeted `lexau build --acts "<name>" --force` re-convert of that set, or fold it
into the next scheduled growth/freshness re-ingest. The 1-line guard is committed
and regression-tested, so every future build is correct.
